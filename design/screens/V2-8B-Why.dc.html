How it works, in plain English Evidence tiers: A, FDA-authorized health claim · B, strong research · C, early research.

AFDA-authorized claim
White beans: soluble fiber that forms a gel. In your gut, the gel traps bile acids, which your liver makes from cholesterol. Those bile acids leave your body instead of being recycled, so your liver pulls LDL cholesterol from your blood to make more.1

AFDA-authorized claim
Kale and whole-grain bread: more fiber. Fiber from vegetables and whole grains adds up across the week, and whole grains take the place of refined bread.1

BStrong research
Olive oil instead of butter: less saturated fat. Saturated fat is the fat most tied to higher LDL. Olive oil's fats are mostly unsaturated, so this dinner stays at about 3 g per serving.2

The official word

"Diets low in saturated fat and cholesterol and rich in fruits, vegetables and grain products that contain some types of dietary fiber, particularly soluble fiber, may reduce the risk of heart disease, a disease associated with many factors."1

One dinner won't change a lab result on its own. A week of meals like it is the point.

Bonus pairing

Lemon + beans and kale. Lemon juice adds vitamin C, which helps your body absorb the plant iron in beans and kale.3 That's a bonus, not part of your LDL goal.
